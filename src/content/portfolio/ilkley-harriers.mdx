## A Club With No Bottleneck

Ilkley Harriers is Ilkley's inclusive running club for all ages and abilities. Tuesday club nights, hill sessions on Thursdays, a busy junior section for ages 8 to 16, and four races of its own every year, including the Ilkley Moor Fell Race and the Pete Shields 10k.

For more than twenty years the club's website was a labour of love. A former Harrier built it, their son Sam took it on, and Sam's mum looked after a separate junior site. It was a real family affair, and it held the club's history going back to 1999.

It also meant every change queued behind one busy person. Race reports had no web address of their own, so nobody could share them. Uploading a photo meant waiting for every previous image to load first. Updating the race league meant emailing the changes to the one editor who could make them.

**So I moved the club onto a WordPress website that any Harrier can update, brought juniors and seniors together, and carried the history across with it.**

## What I Built

A club website where the volunteers who know the content are the ones who update it.

### News That Travels

- **Every report gets its own page**: more than 1,700 news posts and race reports, back to 2021, each with a link that can be shared anywhere
- **Major announcements**: up to three sticky posts sit at the top of the homepage for the big news, and the section disappears when there aren't any
- **Photographer credits built in**: a caption added in the media library appears under the photo wherever it's used, so photographers get their credit
- **One inbox for news**: members send results and photos to one club address, and the news team turns them into posts

### The Club's Own Races

- **A page for each race**: Ilkley Moor Fell Race, Ilkley Trail Race, Pete Shields 10k and Ilkley Incline, each with race details, sponsors and a results archive
- **Ordered by date**: the homepage lists races by their start date, so the next one is always first
- **Race League**: rules, the season's races with their type, date and results, and the league's history, with a new season set by changing one field

### Records and Documents Without a Developer

- **Club Records**: committee members add age categories, distances and new records themselves
- **Committee, AGM and policy documents**: upload a file, choose who it's for and the year, and it appears on the right page automatically
- **Junior notices**: the same system puts notices on the juniors' homepage

### Juniors and Seniors Under One Roof

- **One site, two audiences**: Junior Ilkley Harriers has its own section for training times, races, notices and joining, linked from the homepage
- **Shared news**: junior and senior reports appear together on the homepage, so the whole club sees what everyone is up to

### A Clean Handover

- **The old site, kept**: everything from the previous site lives on at an archive address, with a link from the club menu
- **Domain and email moved**: the club's domain and email addresses moved across, with forwarding set up for each role
- **Training and a runbook**: sessions with the senior and junior editors, plus a written guide for every common task, from adding a race result to setting up a new committee member

## Looking Ahead

The site went live in July 2026, and the club has been running it ever since. The committee keeps a shared list of improvements, and I work through it with them: a relays page, an annual awards page each January, and more for the junior cross-country leagues.

---

## Key Takeaways

For any club, charity or community group run by volunteers:

1. **Remove the single point of failure**: if one person holds the keys to the website, everyone waits on them
2. **Give every story its own link**: members can share a race report the day it goes up
3. **Build for the people who'll update it**: forms with clear fields beat page builders for volunteers who edit once a month
4. **Keep the history, but don't let it hold you back**: bring across what members use and archive the rest
