## How to use a brief

1. Pick the next brief.
2. **Steve writes or dictates the substance.** Every post needs one real thing only Steve can supply: a client story, a number from a project, a mistake he's seen, an opinion. The brief says what to bring under "From Steve". The agent must not invent clients, numbers or quotes.
3. Paste it to the blog agent with the brief's number, e.g. "Brief 1, here's my draft". The agent uses the brief's search title, description, category and links.

## What the research says

- **Cost questions are the biggest gap.** "How much does a small business website cost UK" and "website maintenance cost per month UK" are asked constantly, and the pages ranking are small UK agencies like yours, not big brands. Nothing on the site answers either.
- **Care plans have the thinnest coverage.** Website Support, now a main offer, has 2 posts out of 18.
- **Rescue searches are specific and winnable**: "my web designer disappeared", "how to get my domain back from…", "website hacked what to do". Small UK sites rank for all of them.
- **Platform comparisons** (Wix vs WordPress, Squarespace vs WordPress) attract owners who already have a site and are unhappy. That's the Rescue audience.
- **Local trust searches** ("how to get more Google reviews", Google Business Profile) bring in the kind of owner who needs an audit, and they back up the reviews post published in September.

## Briefs

### 1. What a small business website costs in the UK

- **Search title:** `Small Business Website Cost UK (2026) | BVS Web Design`
- **Slug:** `small-business-website-cost-uk`
- **Category:** Website Design
- **Must answer:** How much should I pay for a small business website? Why are quotes so different (£300 vs £2,000 vs £10,000)? What are the ongoing costs (domain, hosting, maintenance)? What does a cheap site leave out?
- **From Steve:** real price ranges you see, what moves a quote up or down, one example of a cheap build that cost more later. Builds start at £2,000; say why.
- **Links:** /services/website-design/ (main), /services/website-support/ (ongoing costs), /blog/who-owns-my-website/
- **Watch out:** don't publish a fixed price list (the /pricing/ page is hidden). Ranges and "it depends on…" with real examples are fine.

## Refreshes

Small edits to existing posts that already have some authority. Update `date:` only if the content changes substantially.

- **R1. `what-to-do-when-my-website-designer-scams-me`.** 694 impressions at position 14.6. People search "my web designer has **disappeared**" and "can't get hold of my web designer" more than "gone silent". Change the search title to `My Web Designer Has Disappeared: What to Do Next`, use both phrases in the opening paragraphs, and link to brief 3's post.
- **R2. `why-is-my-website-traffic-not-bringing-leads`.** It targets "website traffic but no leads" (good) but is filed as Case Studies. Move it to `Website Audit`, set the search title to `Website Traffic but No Leads? Here's Why`, and link to /website-not-generating-leads/ so the two don't compete.
- **R3. `the-5-biggest-mistakes…`, `5-essential-tips…` and `these-5-sales-boosting-tips…`** are 2025 posts with stock theme images and generic advice. Merge the best parts into one stronger post (e.g. "Small business website mistakes that cost you enquiries"), keep the strongest URL, and add redirects from the other two in `public/_redirects`. Do this with Steve, not automatically.
- **R4. `why-your-business-website-needs-a-care-plan…`.** 351 impressions at position 18.9, competing with the care plans page. Make it match the current Essentials / Active / Growth plans, retarget the search title at "Do I Need a Website Care Plan?" and link prominently to /services/website-support/ so Google treats that page as the main one for "website care plans".
- **R5. `bridal-website-design-maidens-and-ravens`.** Maidens & Ravens is the York proof. Name York in the search title and opening paragraph, and link to /web-designer-york/ (and add the post's link on the York page if it isn't already in the work grid).
